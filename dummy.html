A Pathway to Business Ownership and Residence

The New Zealand Business Investor Work Visa is designed for experienced business people who want to invest in and actively operate an established business in New Zealand. The visa allows eligible applicants to live and work in New Zealand while running the business they invest in. It can also provide a pathway to a Business Investor Resident Visa , depending on the amount invested and how long the business is actively operated. The visa has two main investment options: a standard pathway with an investment of at least NZD $1 million and a fast-track pathway with an investment of at least NZD $2 million . Below is a practical guide covering the key requirements, investment options, business criteria, documents, application process, timeline, family benefits and pathway to residence.

1. What Is the New Zealand Business Investor Work Visa?

The Business Investor Work Visa is a temporary visa for experienced business people who want to invest in and actively run an established New Zealand business. The visa can be valid for up to 4 years . It has two stages:
  • Establishment Stage: The first 12 months, during which you purchase the approved business and begin operating it.
  • Operating Stage: The remaining period, during which you continue to operate and grow the business.
Within the first 9 months , you must provide evidence that you have purchased and started running the business. If the required evidence is not provided or approved, the visa can expire at the end of the 12-month establishment stage. The visa is intended for people who want to be actively involved in the business rather than simply making a passive investment.

2. Who Should Consider the New Zealand Business Investor Work Visa?

This visa may be suitable for experienced entrepreneurs, business owners and senior business professionals who have the capital and experience to acquire and operate an established New Zealand business. It may be worth considering if you:
  • Have significant capital available for business investment
  • Have at least 3 years of acceptable business experience
  • Want to purchase and actively operate an existing NZ business
  • Want to create employment in New Zealand
  • Want to build a long-term business presence in the country
  • Want a potential pathway from business ownership to residence
The visa is not designed for passive investors. You are expected to be actively involved in running the nominated business.

3. Investment Options and Residence Pathways

There are two main investment options.
Feature Standard Pathway Fast-Track Pathway
Minimum investment NZD $1 million NZD $2 million
Residence eligibility After 3 years After 12 months
Business operation Active involvement required Active involvement required
Reserve funds NZD $500,000 NZD $500,000
Total business operation requirement 3 years 3 years in total
With an investment of at least NZD $1 million , you may be eligible to apply for a Business Investor Resident Visa after 3 years of actively running the business. With an investment of at least NZD $2 million , you may be eligible to apply for residence after 12 months. However, the fast-track option does not remove the 3-year business operation requirement. Applicants using the fast-track pathway must still continue running the business for a total of at least 3 years under the applicable residence conditions.

4. Key Eligibility Requirements for Applicants

Applicants must meet several personal, financial and business requirements.

Investment Funds

You must show that you can invest at least:
  • NZD $1 million under the standard pathway, or
  • NZD $2 million under the fast-track pathway.
The investment amount does not include GST, the value of the business property or transaction costs.

Reserve Funds

You must also have at least NZD $500,000 in reserve funds to support yourself and your family, if included, while running and growing the business.

Business Experience

You must demonstrate at least 3 years of acceptable business experience . This can include qualifying self-employment or senior management experience, subject to Immigration New Zealand’s specific requirements.

Age

You must be 55 years old or younger when you apply.

English

The main applicant must meet the English-language requirement. Depending on your circumstances, this may be demonstrated through an approved English test or an accepted English-speaking background.

Health and Character

Applicants must meet New Zealand’s health and character requirements and be considered a fit and proper person.

5. Requirements for the Nominated New Zealand Business

The business you intend to purchase must meet specific requirements. Generally, the nominated business must:
  • Have been actively trading for at least 5 years
  • Be lawfully operating in New Zealand
  • Comply with New Zealand business, employment and immigration standards
  • Have a purchase price of at least NZD $1 million , excluding GST and property value
  • Result in the applicant owning at least 25% of the business
  • Already employ at least 5 full-time equivalent employees
  • Provide a genuine opportunity for the applicant to actively run the business
  • Allow the applicant to create at least 1 new full-time equivalent job for a New Zealand citizen or resident.
The business must also meet restrictions relating to previous ownership and previous use in certain New Zealand investor or entrepreneur visa applications.

6. Buying an Existing Business in New Zealand

Unlike some business migration pathways, the Business Investor Work Visa is focused on investing in an existing established New Zealand business . The applicant can purchase the business outright or acquire a qualifying ownership interest, provided the investment and ownership requirements are met. Before purchasing a business, proper due diligence is important. This may include reviewing:
  • Financial statements
  • Tax records
  • Sales and revenue
  • Employee records
  • Existing contracts
  • Business liabilities
  • Ownership structure
  • Regulatory compliance
  • Business valuation
  • Future growth potential
A business may look attractive commercially but still fail to meet immigration requirements. Therefore, business selection and immigration planning should be considered together.

7. Franchise Businesses Are Now Allowed

One important recent update is that applicants can now invest in franchise businesses , provided the franchise meets the existing Business Investor Work Visa requirements. This change came into effect on 6 July 2026 and expands the range of businesses that investors can consider. Applicants can also purchase the nominated business through a New Zealand resident entity where the relevant requirements are satisfied. Another important change allows qualifying lawfully gifted funds or assets to be used for the investment, subject to Immigration New Zealand’s evidence requirements.

8. Documentation and Due Diligence

Strong documentation is an important part of the application. Applicants may need to provide evidence covering:

Investment Funds

  • Bank statements
  • Property ownership and valuations
  • Share certificates
  • Business ownership records
  • Asset valuations
  • Tax records
  • Evidence showing how the funds were earned or acquired
The funds must generally be owned by the applicant, partner and/or dependent children included in the application and must have been lawfully earned or acquired. Borrowed funds are generally not acceptable, subject to specific rules.

Business Documents

  • Business financial statements
  • Ownership records
  • Purchase agreement
  • Business valuation
  • Employee records
  • Tax and PAYE records
  • Organisational information
  • Evidence of business operations

Professional Due Diligence

The application also requires important professional evidence, including a Business Proposal Form (INZ 1393) and supporting valuation and due-diligence documentation. Financial due diligence must be supported by a qualified statutory accountant, while legal due diligence must be completed by a New Zealand lawyer who meets Immigration New Zealand’s independence requirements.

9. Application Process and Timeline

The process generally involves the following stages: Step 1: Assess eligibility Review your business experience, age, investment funds, English, health and character requirements. Step 2: Identify a qualifying business Find an established New Zealand business that meets the visa requirements. Step 3: Conduct due diligence Review the financial, legal and commercial position of the business. Step 4: Prepare the application Prepare the business proposal, financial evidence, ownership documents, business records and personal documents. Step 5: Submit the Business Investor Work Visa application Submit the application and supporting evidence to Immigration New Zealand. Step 6: Purchase and begin operating the business After approval and under the applicable visa conditions, complete the approved business purchase and begin actively running the business. Step 7: Complete the establishment milestone Within the first 9 months, provide evidence showing that the business has been purchased and that you have started running it. Step 8: Continue operating the business Maintain the required ownership, staffing, employment, financial and regulatory conditions. Step 9: Apply for the Business Investor Resident Visa Once you meet the applicable residence pathway requirements, you can apply for the Business Investor Resident Visa. Processing times can vary depending on the application, evidence, due diligence and Immigration New Zealand processing capacity.

10. Visa Duration and Renewal

The Business Investor Work Visa can be valid for up to 4 years . The first 12 months are the Establishment Stage. The remaining period is the Operating Stage. If you need additional time to meet the requirements for residence, you may be able to receive one further Business Investor Work Visa of up to 2 years , subject to the applicable requirements.

11. Family Members

The Business Investor Work Visa allows applicants to include their partner and dependent children in the application. The Business Investor Resident Visa can also include a partner and dependent children aged 24 or younger , subject to the applicable dependency requirements. This can make the pathway relevant for business owners who want to relocate to New Zealand with their immediate family.

12. How Does the Business Investor Work Visa Lead to Residence?

The Business Investor Work Visa can lead to the Business Investor Resident Visa .

Standard Pathway

If you invest at least NZD $1 million, you may apply for residence after actively running the business for at least 3 years .

Fast-Track Pathway

If you invest at least NZD $2 million, you may apply for residence after actively running the business for at least 12 months . However, fast-track applicants must still operate the business for at least 3 years in total to satisfy the conditions attached to their resident visa. To meet the residence requirements, applicants must also maintain the required employment, ownership, business compliance, financial and New Zealand presence conditions. For example, Immigration New Zealand requires applicants to have spent at least 184 days per year in New Zealand while meeting the relevant Business Investor Work Visa conditions.

13. From Resident Visa to Permanent Residence

The Business Investor Resident Visa allows you to stay in New Zealand indefinitely. After holding the Business Investor Resident Visa for at least 2 years and meeting the applicable conditions, you may be able to apply for a Permanent Resident Visa . This means the overall pathway can be viewed as: Business Investor Work Visa → Business Investor Resident Visa → Permanent Resident Visa The exact timing depends on the investment pathway, business operation and fulfilment of all immigration conditions.

14. Common Reasons for Application Problems

Applications can face delays, requests for additional evidence or refusal when important requirements are not properly demonstrated. Common issues may include:
  • Insufficient evidence of investment funds
  • Unclear or unsupported source of funds
  • Business not meeting the required criteria
  • Weak financial due diligence
  • Problems with business valuation
  • Insufficient business experience evidence
  • Failure to demonstrate active business involvement
  • Weak evidence of employment
  • Failure to meet the new job-creation requirement
  • Incomplete documentation
  • Failure to meet health or character requirements
  • Failure to provide establishment-stage evidence within the required period
Careful preparation and professional due diligence can help reduce avoidable problems.

15. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum investment for the New Zealand Business Investor Work Visa?

The minimum investment is NZD $1 million . A NZD $2 million investment can provide access to the fast-track residence pathway.

How much reserve money is required?

Applicants must generally show at least NZD $500,000 in reserve funds in addition to the investment amount.

Can I buy a franchise?

Yes. From 6 July 2026 , qualifying franchise businesses can be considered under the Business Investor Work Visa.

Can I use gifted funds?

Lawfully gifted funds or assets can now be used where the applicable requirements are satisfied and the lawful source can be demonstrated.

Can I include my family?

Yes. You can include your partner and dependent children, subject to the applicable requirements.

Is the Business Investor Work Visa a permanent resident visa?

No. It is a temporary work visa that can provide a pathway to the Business Investor Resident Visa. The resident visa allows indefinite stay, and eligible holders may later apply for permanent residence.

Can I start a completely new business?

The current Business Investor Work Visa is focused on investing in and actively running an established New Zealand business . The nominated business must meet Immigration New Zealand’s requirements.

Conclusion

The New Zealand Business Investor Work Visa provides an opportunity for experienced business people to invest in and actively operate an established New Zealand business. The pathway offers two main investment options: NZD $1 million for the standard 3-year route to residence or NZD $2 million for the 12-month fast-track route , while the fast-track applicant still needs to operate the business for at least 3 years in total under the applicable conditions. Recent changes have also expanded investment flexibility, including qualifying franchise businesses, purchases through New Zealand resident entities and certain lawfully gifted funds. For entrepreneurs considering New Zealand business migration, the most important steps are choosing the right business, conducting proper financial and legal due diligence, preparing strong evidence and understanding the visa conditions before making the investment.
